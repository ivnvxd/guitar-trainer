# Research behind the drills

Two research passes on 2026-11-10 set the design of the drills. Items marked [J] are design judgment, not a finding. Numbers in brackets point to the source lists below.

## Ear training

1. Interleaved identification with adaptive review. Mix the active intervals over random roots. Each question plays at once and takes one key press. Missed items and confused pairs come back sooner. Interleaving beat blocked practice, although learners preferred blocks [E1]. EarMaster repeats weak items [E15].
2. Add a new interval at about 80 to 90 percent accuracy [E8, E18]. A correct answer slower than about 3 seconds still counts as unlearned [J].
3. After a miss, play the chosen and the correct interval on the same root, twice [J]. Ask the item again 3 to 5 questions later [J]. Banacos alternates the wrong and the right pitch until both are clear [E8]. Exposure blocks added to practice helped [E2].
4. Sing first, then answer. Practitioners agree on the value of the voice [E18, E12]. Songs are a shortcut and weak for harmonic intervals [E18, E19].
5. Intervals and scale degrees in a key. A cadence sets the key, then one note or two notes follow [E9, E10, E11, E12]. The note resolves to the tonic after the answer [E8, E12]. Change keys often [E21].
6. Call and response on the guitar: hear the interval, find the second note on the neck [E7, E20].
7. Order: P5, M3, octave, P4, then m3, M2, m2, then M6, m6, tritone, then m7, M7 [J, E9]. Ascending first, then descending, then harmonic [E3, E16, E18].
8. Dose: about 10 minutes a day [E10, E12]. Sessions over an hour overtrained in one study [E5].
9. Avoid long single-interval blocks, waiting for 100 percent, harmonic intervals first, and a bare "wrong, next" [E1, E3, E8, E18].

## Neck and harmony fluency

1. Name the note at a dot, with time per answer per fret [F2]. One string or a fret range first [F1].
2. One note on all strings: find every F♯ on frets 0 to 12 [F1, F3].
3. Triads: find a named triad and inversion on a string set. Later, find the closest inversion for the next chord [F6, F7, F8, F9].
4. CAGED: name the two shapes that have their root on a given string [F11]. Name a shown shape [F10]. Keep CAGED a map, not a goal [F12].
5. Keys: mixed facts such as "V of E♭", "relative minor of D♭" or "flats in A♭" [F13]. Diatonic chords such as "ii in E" [F4].
6. Progressions: numbers to chords in any key, with accuracy, time and a review of misses [F14, F15].
7. Random order was slower in practice but better at a retest two days later [F23]. Spacing helped recall [F21]. Recall from memory, then check [F24].
